Add model 5: wages, indexation and the spiral gain

A fifth model for the site, asking the inflation question from the other
side: when prices move, what happens to what households are paid, and does
that feed back into prices?

Two objects. The Effective Wage Index adds statutory wage floors, indexed
benefits and one-off handouts to market pay, weighted by an income group's
actual composition, and deflates both by the recorded price index and by the
index that would have been recorded without caps and tariff shields. The
Spiral Gain G = Lambda x M is the product of price-to-wage catch-up and
wage-to-price pass-through.

The indexation intensity lambda is built from a hand-assembled, row-by-row
sourced database of who was indexed to what across seven countries from 1960
(config/indexation_coverage.csv): contractual escalators, statutory public
pay, minimum-wage-benchmarked agreements and formal inflation references.

The wage equation is PARTIALLY POOLED. A Chow test rejects one coefficient
vector for seven countries (F = 2.87 on (120, 1030), p = 3e-19; own-lag
persistence runs from 0.967 in the US to 0.222 in Italy), so every country
keeps its own persistence, trend weight and slack response. Only the catch-up
level and its interaction with lambda are common, because lambda barely moves
inside a country and the cross-section is the only place that effect is
identified. Homogeneity binds once per country.

Freeing the dynamics cuts the interaction from +0.339 to +0.110: two-thirds
of what a fully pooled specification attributed to indexation was pooled-
dynamics misspecification. What survives is the sign — dLambda/dlambda is
positive in all seven countries, +0.102 to +0.364 — and that is now the
headline rather than the magnitude. The level of Lambda is persistence-
dominated and not comparable across countries; G is negative in all seven
today. Belgium pairs the highest lambda in the panel with M = -0.083, which
is why its indexation never produced excess inflation.

Two further specification decisions are load-bearing and documented in
docs/DECISIONS.md: the panel is estimated on four-quarter growth rates
because annualised quarterly growth of non-ECI wage series is close to white
noise, and the catch-up term is a one-sided real-wage gap rather than
Bernanke & Blanchard's inflation surprise, which is collinear with a filtered
trend by construction and returned coefficient sums of +54.

Includes: keyless data clients for FRED, ONS, Eurostat and INSEE; an
employment-weighted effective US minimum wage; statutory benefit uprating
rules implemented and validated against published decisions; a parity-tested
browser twin; the interactive page; and the paper, with a house-style HTML
build and a LaTeX PDF.
